# UK cost-of-living benchmarks (starting points — refresh each run)

These are the comparison figures used in the review. **They go stale** (price
caps change quarterly, the ONS/JRF numbers update yearly), so re-search them
each time rather than copying blind. Last gathered: **November 2026**. Use
`WebSearch` and cite the live source in the PDF's Sources list.

## What to look up each run

| Benchmark | Search for | Nov-2026 figure (verify) |
|---|---|---|
| Average household total spend | "ONS Family spending in the UK" latest | ~£676.60/week (FYE2025) ≈ £2,930/mo; NimbleFins 2026 est ~£717/wk ≈ £3,100/mo |
| Energy price cap | "Ofgem energy price cap <quarter> <year>" | Oct–Dec 2026: £1,723/yr ≈ £144/mo (typical dual-fuel DD) |
| Family-of-4 groceries | "average UK family of four food shopping cost <year>" | ~£550–£900/mo food shopping |
| Food out / takeaways | "average UK household spend eating out takeaways <year>" | ~£50–55/mo takeaways; ~£200/mo total food-out for a family of 4 |
| Income adequacy | "Joseph Rowntree Minimum Income Standard <year> couple two children" | 2026: ~£77,400/yr combined — **but assumes two earners + childcare**; a single-earner household with a stay-at-home parent needs less (no childcare) |

## Framing notes

- The ONS "average household" is ~2.4 people, so a **family of four running above
  average is expected** — compare the *shape* (which categories are high), not
  just the total.
- The JRF figure assumes **two working parents with childcare costs**. For a
  single breadwinner with no childcare bill, don't present it as a shortfall;
  use it to show the income is adequate and the issue is allocation.
- Always caveat that the review is built from **one month**, which can be skewed
  by quarterly/annual bills (water, car tax, insurance) and one-offs.

## Canonical sources (check they still resolve)

- ONS Family spending in the UK — ons.gov.uk
- Ofgem energy price cap — ofgem.gov.uk / Uswitch / EDF energywise
- NimbleFins / ClearScore — average UK household budget & food cost
